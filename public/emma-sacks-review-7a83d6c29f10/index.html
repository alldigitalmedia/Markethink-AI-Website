A strong eye, an account that goes quiet

The aesthetic is clear and premium. The problem is rhythm and purpose: long silences between posts, a generic bio and a feed that shows the work without the person or the reasons behind it.

What already works

A clear point of view

Warm palette, marble, deep greens and wood, shot to a professional standard.

Named projects

Westchester, Irvington, Harrison and Coral Gables: four complete, well-photographed homes and spaces.

The right highlights

Services, Process and Projects already exist; they only need order and covers.

Format variety

Carousels, reels, renders and a portrait of the founder.

Tagging partners

The Plenitude Med Spa post shows the collaboration habit worth keeping.

Custom furniture

A service on the website that the feed has never shown.

What holds the account back

  • Irregular posting, with gaps of months; one grid piece even says "We're back".
  • A bio that says "timeless interiors" but not who it is for, what it sells or what to do.
  • Almost no presence of Emma: clients buy trust in the designer.
  • Few reels, and not narrative (a marble texture with music).
  • No testimonials, results or before-and-afters.
  • Short captions with no question and no call to action.
  • Tweet-style screenshots that break the editorial grid.
  • Following more accounts than follow back.

Five circles in one style: Projects, Services, Process, About and Contact, with photographic covers from her own projects (option 3 of four in section 04). Three pinned posts: How we work, the Coral Gables Spa reel and the testimonial.

Four content pillars

Every month we reinforce the pillars that bring the most saves, profile visits, link clicks and messages.

House rules for every post

Text on 10% of posts

Full-bleed photography, no text on the image. Only when text is needed (a client quote) does it go on the image. The explanation lives in the caption.

Copy

A hook, the design decision, then a question or a call to action. "We" for the studio, "I" when Emma speaks. English only.

Reels without a camera

Slow pans across real photographs, no text on screen and, when possible, Emma's voice-over recorded on her phone.

Community

Reply to every comment in the first hour. Tag vendors and clients. Comment on local architects, builders and agents.

The thread of the account

Designed with intention

Every room has a reason. We show it.

Who we talk to

Homeowners moving to Miami or New York, buying a second home or renovating, who want to hand off the whole project. Second: the agents, architects and builders who refer them.

How we sound

A calm expert who explains her criteria, not a brand that sells. Concrete material words (honed marble, white oak), few emojis, no hype.

Emma, without the camera

One short shoot a month, voice-overs recorded on her phone, photos of her on site and captions in her own voice.

05 · Why this change

Clients buy trust before they buy a room

Interior design is a high-ticket, high-trust decision. A feed that explains decisions, shows the designer and posts on a steady rhythm earns that trust; a quiet gallery does not.

Consistency is reach

Four posts a week on fixed days tell the algorithm, and the client, that the studio is active.

Reasons get saved

"Why it works" carousels are the content people save and send, which extends reach without paid media.

A face without a camera

Emma's voice in captions and voice-overs builds the personal brand with almost no recording time.

The photography is already there

Four professionally shot projects: the plan starts now, with no new production.

A message no one owns

"Designed from afar" speaks directly to people relocating to Miami or New York.

We measure inquiries

Messages with HOME or MOVE, link clicks and profile visits, not likes.

Today

  • Months without posting.
  • Portfolio only.
  • Short captions, no call to action.
  • Screenshots mixed with editorial photos.

Proposed

  • Four posts a week, on fixed days.
  • Portfolio, reasons, Emma and proof.
  • A question or a consultation in every caption.
  • Photography only; text on ~10% of posts.
